01 / SEARCH BEHAVIOR & PAGE IDENTITY

When the wrong page appears for the right query

A metropolitan law-firm practice page and its parent hub competed for search exposure.

Search data · URL inspection · Technical QA

The question

Was Google favoring a general personal-injury hub over a more specific car-accident page because of a page-identity defect—or because of a broader relevance problem?

My investigative approach

Compared exact queries and URLs across dates, segmented devices and markets, checked indexed canonicals and current page content, and reviewed internal links and change timing before recommending an action.

Observed: hub shown more oftenVerified: both URLs independently indexedUnproven: the hub caused weak rankings
What the evidence established

The search-data preference was reproducible; both pages remained indexed with distinct self-canonicals and intended content. Earlier records showed the specialized page was already weak before the observed switch.

Decision & status

Retain both pages. A targeted contextual link was proposed as a measured test; no redirect, merge, or blanket rewrite was justified. Search-performance causality remains open.

02 / MULTI-PAGE CLUSTER INVESTIGATION

Auditing a city-wide content cluster

Separating actual defects from weak performance signals across a large page group.

Content QA · Redirects · Localization · Schema

The challenge

A city hub and its related service pages had uneven search visibility, complex internal-link paths and questions about whether Google had recognized newer page versions.

My contribution

Organized the investigation by page and evidence source, reviewed destination intent and internal links, distinguished incorrect links from ranking observations, and coordinated bounded, approved corrections with verification records.

Inventory pages→Compare saved & public output→Classify findings→Approve targeted edits→Verify & record
Confirmed findings

The November re-audit reported all ten core pages indexed with matching canonicals, alongside wrong-intent destinations and other localized issues requiring focused handling. A later approved mechanical correction batch was saved and exact-diff checked.

Evidence limits

Some ordinary-browser, cache and mobile checks remained outstanding after the latest batch. Google recognition of the latest versions and any resulting search impact were not proven. The case remains an investigation, not a ranking-recovery claim.

03 / HISTORICAL CHANGE & CAUSAL ANALYSIS

Did an internal-link cleanup cause a decline?

Testing a plausible explanation against actual link-change receipts.

Change logs · Inlink comparisons · Hypothesis testing

The hypothesis

A previous link cleanup may have removed inbound references to an important city-specific car-accident page, contributing to a later visibility decline.

How I tested it

Reconciled historical source-and-destination rows, distinguished direct target links from generic redirects, compared two crawl inlink exports, and preserved unknown crawl dates and incomplete historical redirect evidence.

Hypothesis: valuable inlinks were removedReceipts: four examined changes pointed to the targetOutcome: direct-removal explanation not confirmed
Key investigative finding

The examined exact-destination change-log rows documented links directed to the target page—not removals from it. Differences between crawl exports could not be interpreted as losses without reliable crawl dates and comparable scope.

Decision & status

No unsupported rollback. The investigation stayed open for historical redirect confirmation and other possible causes. The value here is ruling out an attractive but unproven explanation.

04 / PERSISTENT CRAWL & INDEXING DISCREPANCY

When a page works—but its search record stays stale

A three-page investigation across retrieval, rendering, indexing and deployment history.

HTTP · Rendering · Canonicals · Cache · GSC

The problem

Several service pages loaded and rendered successfully in current tests, yet Google’s stored crawl and indexing records remained old or excluded. The pages did not all share the same indexing status.

Investigation method

Checked current URLs and redirects, robots and canonicals, sitemap inclusion, rendered HTML, WordPress duplicates, caching, staging exposure, and dated Google inspections. Kept current retrieval separate from historical index records.

Current public retrieval: successfulStored Google records: old / excludedRoot cause: not yet established
What was established

Tested pages served their intended content and survived rendering. One page was indexed with an old reported crawl; two were excluded with older crawl records. Other pages in the same area were being crawled.

Next decision

Avoid another speculative rewrite or URL change. Complete remaining account-level checks and use the prepared evidence for an indexing escalation. The report was drafted, not recorded as submitted.

MY INVESTIGATION STANDARD

A useful answer is one you can trace back to evidence.
