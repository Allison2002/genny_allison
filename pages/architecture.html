01 / LARGE-SCALE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Thompson Law

From inconsistent geographic URLs to a defined page hierarchy.

Website architecture · Migration planning

The challenge

A large legal website expanded across states and cities without a consistently expressed geographic hierarchy. Older URL patterns varied by city, state, and practice, making page relationships, mapping, and location-based reporting difficult to manage.

My contribution

Researched and helped establish a national → state → city/office → practice-area structure; worked on URL mapping and redirect planning; coordinated internal-link priorities and technical validation across the migration.

National website↓State hub /tx/↓City / office hub /tx/dallas/↓City practice page /tx/dallas/car-accident-lawyers/
Design decisions

Consistent geographic paths, clear parent/child intent, documented legacy mapping, and validation of redirects, canonicals, navigation and internal linking.

Implementation context

The geographic hierarchy and migration planning are documented, but subsequent performance was affected by overlapping site changes and reported server, crawling, and caching incidents. No single cause of the search decline or ranking recovery is established by this case study.

02 / MULTI-LOCATION HEALTHCARE

Premier Injury Clinics

Reorganizing what already exists before creating more.

Audit review · Content systems

The challenge

An outside audit recommended more location pages and substantially more copy. My review of the existing site found location pages and extensive content were already present, but page overlap, weak organization and inconsistent visitor pathways called for a different priority.

My contribution

Reviewed the audit against existing pages; separated useful recommendations from unsupported assumptions; proposed a structure built around locations, treatment services and injuries, supported by blogs and contextual links.

Locations WHEREServices HOWInjuries WHAT

Supporting articles connect visitors to the relevant location, treatment, and injury information.

Recommended sequence

Audit existing URLs → decide which pages to retain or consolidate → finalize redirects and primary destinations → organize internal links → expand only for verified gaps.

Evaluation limitations

The audit review and architecture proposal are documented. The site underwent repeated design changes, limiting the time available to measure any one version consistently. Early improvement is reported, but its extent and attribution require dated change and Search Console comparisons.

03 / HANDS-ON WEBSITE DEVELOPMENT

Owl Pipe ID

Hands-on ownership in an established WordPress site, with later search visibility to investigate.

WordPress · UI components · Forms

The challenge

Extend an existing business website without making the new experience feel disconnected from the site's established structure and visual conventions.

My contribution

Worked within the WordPress environment to add a Free Samples page and form, reuse familiar site components, and maintain continuity with the navigation and existing layouts.

Approach

Study the existing components → identify the page's role → build within reusable patterns → check content and form behavior.

Observed visibility · not causal attribution

In a six-month Google Search Console view shared November 2026, the website recorded 18.2K impressions, 167 clicks and an average position of 16.9. The chart shows a later increase in visibility; it does not isolate the effect of this page/form work or establish lead conversions.

04 / OPERATIONAL SYSTEM DESIGN

Vito / Lucky OS

Designing technical workflows that preserve evidence and require validation.

AI-assisted workflows · QA · Human approval

The challenge

Recurring audits and investigations produce large amounts of evidence, interrupted work, competing hypotheses and proposed changes. Without a durable process, it's easy to repeat work or mistake a prepared change for a verified fix.

My contribution

Defined an investigation and operations workflow emphasizing source traceability, checkpoints, change status, review gates, and clear separation between analysis and production implementation.

Collect evidence→Investigate→Recommend→Approve & implement→Verify & log
Core guardrails

Keep observations distinct from hypotheses, mark unresolved findings honestly, retain recovery checkpoints, and require authorization before live changes.

Current status

Ongoing system design and development. The workflow documentation supports the design approach, not a claim of fully autonomous production deployment.
